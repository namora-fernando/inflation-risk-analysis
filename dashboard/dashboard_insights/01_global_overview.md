# Global Inflation Risk Overview (2024)

This page provides a high-level snapshot of global inflation risk conditions in the latest available year, **2024**. It combines geographic distribution, country rankings, and long-term trends to answer one core question:

*Where are inflation risks currently concentrated, and how severe are they relative to history?*

---

## Purpose of This Page

The objective of this page is to:
- Summarize the overall global inflation risk environment in 2024.
- Identify countries with the highest relative risk.
- Show how today’s risk compares with long-term historical patterns.

This page is designed as the main entry point of the dashboard before moving into country-level and regional-level analysis.

## Key Metrics Snapshot (2024)

- **Average Global Risk Score:** 36.03  
  This suggests that, on average, the world is currently in a moderate inflation risk environment.

- **Highest Risk Country:** South Sudan  
  South Sudan records the highest composite inflation risk score in 2024.

- **Share of Countries in High Risk Category:** 25.80%  
  Roughly one quarter of all observed countries fall into the high-risk group.

- **Number of Countries Covered:** 216  
  The analysis spans a broad global coverage.

These headline numbers indicate that while global inflation risk is not uniformly extreme, a substantial portion of countries still face elevated pressure.

---

## Geographic Distribution of Inflation Risk

The global map shows a clear clustering of high-risk countries in:
- Sub-Saharan Africa
- Parts of the Middle East
- Several fragile economies in Asia and Latin America

Low-risk and medium-risk countries are more densely concentrated in:
- North America
- Large parts of Europe
- Selected East Asian economies

This spatial pattern highlights that inflation risk is not evenly distributed. It tends to be structurally higher in regions with weaker macroeconomic stability, higher currency volatility, and stronger supply-side constraints.

The Top 10 highest-risk countries in 2024 are dominated by:
- South Sudan
- Lebanon
- Sudan
- Turkey
- Nigeria
- Egypt
- Zambia
- Malawi
- Iran
- Angola

These countries share several common economic characteristics:
- High inflation persistence
- Exchange rate instability

- Exposure to political or fiscal stress

The ranking confirms that extreme inflation risk is typically concentrated in economies facing both monetary and structural vulnerabilities.

---

## Long-Term Global Risk Trend (1960–2024)

The global average risk score over time shows:
- Cyclical fluctuations rather than a persistent upward trend.
- Periods of elevated risk around:
  - The 1970s and early 1980s
  - The late 2000s
  - The post-2020 period

While the 2024 global average does not sit at historical extremes, it remains elevated relative to several low-risk periods in the 1990s and early 2000s. This suggests that the global inflation environment has not fully normalized after recent economic shocks.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Global inflation risk in 2024 is moderate on average but unevenly distributed.
- Roughly one in four countries is classified as high risk.
- High-risk countries are heavily concentrated in Africa and parts of the Middle East.
- Long-term trends show recurring inflation risk cycles rather than a permanently rising pattern.
- The current global inflation environment remains structurally fragile compared with historical low-risk periods.
